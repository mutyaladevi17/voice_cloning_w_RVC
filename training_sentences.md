# RVC Voice Training Sentences

This dataset contains a balanced set of sentences to record for training a custom voice clone using Retrieval-based Voice Conversion (RVC). It covers descriptive text, philosophical reflections, questions, numbers spoken as words, conversational dialogue, short expressions, technical/scientific prose, and emotional/lyrical passages to capture a wide range of vocal characteristics (timbre, pitch, tone, pace, and intonation).

For best results, record these in a quiet environment using a high-quality microphone, speaking naturally.

---

## 1. Declarative — Descriptive

1. The old lighthouse stood at the edge of the world, its beam sweeping the fog in slow, patient arcs.
2. The forest was quiet in a way that felt deliberate, as though the trees themselves were listening.
3. She climbed the spiral staircase every evening at dusk, her boots ringing on the iron steps.
4. The city hummed with the kind of noise that people stop hearing after the first week.
5. Morning arrived the way it always does — too early for some, and too late for others.
6. The kettle began to whistle just as she found the letter she had been searching for all week.
7. He set the box on the table, very carefully, as if it contained something that could not be replaced.
8. She pressed her palm against the cold glass and watched her breath fog the view of the garden.
9. The dog sat at the end of the hall and watched the door with a patience that bordered on wisdom.
10. She unwrapped the package slowly, as if the paper itself was part of the gift.
11. He walked into the room, stopped, and looked around with the expression of someone who had found something they were not looking for.
12. The light, when it finally came, was nothing like what any of them had imagined — softer, slower, and somehow, more real.
13. It was the kind of afternoon that made you believe time had agreed to slow down, just for you.
14. She laughed so suddenly and so completely that everyone in the room turned to look.
15. He spoke quietly, but every word landed with the precision of something long considered.
16. The river had been rising since Thursday, and by Sunday morning it had crossed the first wall.
17. She cooked the same meal every Friday — not out of habit, but out of devotion.
18. Nothing in the kitchen had changed since nineteen ninety-eight: the same blue tiles, the same cracked mug.
19. He ran toward the station without looking back, his coat streaming behind him in the wind.

## 2. Declarative — Reflective / Philosophical

1. There is a kind of courage that looks exactly like standing still.
2. Every map is wrong in exactly the ways that matter most to the person holding it.
3. The answer, it turned out, had been written on the back of the question the whole time.
4. He counted the stars — not to find the number, but to remind himself that numbers weren't everything.
5. What we call silence is usually just the noise of things we have learned to ignore.
6. Sometimes a candle in the window is not a warning, and not a beacon.
7. It is just a way of saying: I was here, you were here, and the light was enough.
8. Even in the middle of the crowd, there are moments when you are completely, finally alone.
9. The old professor always said the same thing at the start of every term: a question is a door.
10. There are things you carry for years before you realize they were never yours to carry.

## 3. Questions

1. Have you ever watched the sea at night and wondered what it remembers?
2. Could you imagine waking up to find the world had rearranged itself while you slept?
3. Is it possible to miss a place you have never actually been?
4. Why does the thing you lost always turn up in the last place you look?
5. How strange it is that the things we carry in our hands are so much lighter than the ones we carry in our minds.
6. What would you do if you woke up tomorrow and everything you thought you knew had simply — shifted?
7. She knew three languages fluently, and in all of them she told the same story differently — but which version was true?

## 4. Numbers Spoken as Words

1. Forty-two ships had wrecked on those rocks — forty-two, and not one more, since the light was built.
2. Three short blasts: that was the signal for a ship in distress.
3. One thousand, two hundred miles of open water — for a man of seventy-three, that was a story worth hearing.
4. Rain in nineteen eighty-four was different, Thomas insisted — heavier, colder, less forgiving.
5. Twenty-seven years is a long time to keep a secret, and an even longer time to forget one.
6. Five hundred and twelve steps from her front door to the bakery — she had counted them twice.
7. The bridge was built in nineteen twelve, and it had survived everything the river had thrown at it since.
8. She had memorized eighty-three constellations by the time she was twelve years old.
9. The last train departed at eleven forty-five, and she had exactly three minutes to reach the platform.
10. He had promised to be back by four o'clock, and she had been checking the window since three-thirty.
11. There are exactly forty-eight tiles on the ceiling of the waiting room — she had counted them many times.
12. It takes approximately eight minutes and twenty seconds for sunlight to travel from the sun to the earth.
13. Seven billion people share this planet, and somehow I keep running into you at the coffee shop.
14. The numbers did not lie: three hundred and twenty people, forty-two rooms, and not a single empty bed.

## 5. Dialogue / Conversational

1. Turn left at the stone wall, walk for about two hundred meters, and look for the oak tree that split in the storm.
2. The recipe called for two cups of flour, one tablespoon of sugar, and just a pinch of something she couldn't name.
3. They met on a Thursday in November, seventeen years ago, outside a bookshop that no longer exists.
4. The photograph was taken in the summer of two thousand and three, before any of them knew what was coming.
5. The news came through at half past nine on a Friday, which somehow made it harder.

## 7. Science / Technical

1. The human brain contains approximately eighty-six billion neurons, each connected to thousands of others.
2. Water freezes at zero degrees Celsius under standard atmospheric pressure — a fact elegant in its simplicity.
3. The speed of sound through air at sea level is roughly three hundred and forty-three metres per second.
4. Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy, producing oxygen as a byproduct that we breathe.
5. Every element on the periodic table was forged inside a star, including every atom in your body.
6. A single strand of DNA, fully unspooled, would stretch approximately two metres from end to end.
7. The immune system identifies and destroys millions of abnormal cells every day without you ever knowing.
8. Light behaves as both a wave and a particle depending on how and when you choose to observe it.
9. The Milky Way galaxy contains between one hundred billion and four hundred billion stars.
10. Continental drift moves the tectonic plates by roughly the same rate that your fingernails grow each year.

## 8. Procedural / Instructional

1. First, bring the water to a rolling boil; then add the pasta and stir immediately to prevent sticking.
2. Hold the pen lightly — tension travels up through the hand and into the wrist faster than you expect.
3. Press the dough until it springs back slowly, cover it with a cloth, and leave it for exactly one hour.
4. The key to a good photograph is not equipment but patience — wait for the light to do what you need.
5. Start from the outside edge and work inward, removing one screw at a time with steady, even pressure.
6. Before you speak in a difficult room, take one breath — not a dramatic pause, just one quiet breath.
7. Layer the colours from lightest to darkest, letting each dry completely before applying the next.
8. Fold the letter in thirds, not in half: it shows the reader you know how a formal document is meant to look.

## 9. Emotional / Personal

1. She had expected grief to arrive loudly, but it came in small, quiet moments — a mug, a song, a name.
2. He never spoke about the years he spent abroad, but his hands gave him away every time someone mentioned the sea.
3. There is a particular kindness in pretending not to notice when someone is trying very hard not to cry.
4. The letter she had written but never sent still sat in the drawer — folded, addressed, entirely honest.
5. She described it as a feeling of being both full and empty at the same time, which he understood perfectly.
6. The hardest apology is the one you give to yourself, long after everyone else has already forgiven you.
7. He kept every birthday card his children had ever made, even the ones with the spelling mistakes — especially those.
8. It is possible to miss someone every day and still know, clearly, that you made the right decision.

## 10. Historical / Factual

1. The Great Wall of China was not built all at once, but across many centuries and by many different rulers.
2. The printing press, invented in the fourteen-fifties, made it possible for ordinary people to own books for the first time.
3. Marie Curie remains the only person to have won Nobel Prizes in two different scientific disciplines.
4. The first transatlantic telegram was sent in eighteen fifty-eight, and its message took sixteen and a half hours to transmit.
5. Ancient Rome had a population of over one million people at its height, a figure no European city would match for another thousand years.
6. The library of Alexandria, before it was lost, may have held as many as four hundred thousand scrolls.
7. In nineteen sixty-nine, the Apollo eleven mission carried three astronauts toward the moon — but only two of them actually landed.
8. Florence Nightingale transformed nursing into a respected profession by insisting on statistics, clean water, and dignity for patients.

## 11. Comparative / Analytical

1. The difference between a good decision and a lucky one is not always visible until much later.
2. Speed and accuracy rarely coexist in the same measure; most crafts require you to choose which matters more today.
3. The two arguments were logically identical, but one of them felt wrong in a way she could not immediately articulate.
4. A map and a landscape are two entirely different things, and the confusion between them is the source of most arguments about direction.
5. Reading a book twice is not the same experience — you are a different reader the second time through.
6. She preferred the old edition — not because it was better, but because it carried the smell of the room where she first read it.

## 12. Humour / Irony

1. He claimed he had read the manual, which was technically true if you count the copyright page.
2. The sign said the café had been open since nineteen forty-two, which the coffee suggested was also the last time they changed the blend.
3. She said she was almost ready, which in the language of their shared history meant approximately twenty-three minutes.
4. He described himself as a morning person, by which he meant he was deeply unpleasant until his third cup of coffee.
5. The instructions assured the user that assembly would take only fifteen minutes, a claim that assumed four hands and no sense of humour.

## 13. Poetry-like / Lyrical

1. Rain in autumn sounds different from rain in spring — one is letting go, the other is arriving.
2. The stars do not care whether you are watching, but somehow they always look better when you do.
3. Silence between two people who trust each other is the most comfortable thing in the world.
4. Every ending in a story is also a beginning — it just depends on which page you choose to stop reading.
5. The moon was so bright that night it cast shadows, and she stood in one for a long time just to feel it.

## 14. More Questions

1. What is the difference between knowing something and believing it — and does that difference matter?
2. If you could read the last page of any book before you started it, would you?
3. Do you ever wonder what it sounds like from the other side of a conversation you assumed was private?
4. Have you noticed that the things you regret most are rarely the things you did — but the things you did not?
5. When was the last time you sat somewhere completely quiet and did nothing at all, without feeling guilty about it?
